# Adversarial Audit — 6 September 2026

**Scope:** functional spec draft 3.1, technical design draft 1, implementation plan draft 1,
README, open-questions register, research 01–04.

Draft 3.1 was itself an audit, but a *mechanical* one: it checked for dangling references,
duplicate IDs and requirements with no acceptance criteria — that is, for **missing** coverage.
This pass assumed the documents were complete and asked instead whether they were **right**:
where do two statements contradict each other, where does an acceptance criterion test
something other than the requirement it cites, where does a design decision quietly overturn a
spec decision, and — the most productive question — **what does the whole document set agree on
that is wrong?**

Forty-one findings. Thirteen are substantive, three of those are defects no document had
noticed at all, and one is a design bug that would have broken a mechanism half the
architecture rests on.

All findings are addressed. Functional spec → draft 3.2, technical design → draft 1.1,
implementation plan → draft 1.1.

---

## The five that matter most

### 1. The eval-fold discipline sealed away the only noise tape · *§14A.2 + AC-6*

§14A.2 step 5: the noise tape goes in **eval only**. Step 8: do not look at the eval fold until
M11, "not for debugging, not for a quick check". Both rules are correct in isolation.

Together they mean **AC-6 cannot be run until the last milestone.** AC-6 is zero accepted
transcripts from pure squelch noise; it is the acceptance gate for F4, the #1 practical failure
mode, and it is the reason M3 exists. Under drafts 3.1/1, the six hallucination controls would
have been built at M3, tuned with no measurement available, carried through M4–M10, and first
measured at M11 — for the highest-risk and most easily-fooled subsystem in the pipeline.

Worse, the incentive it creates is exactly the one §14A.2 was written to prevent: a developer
who cannot measure hallucination control any other way will eventually open the eval fold, and
one lapse invalidates every number the project publishes.

**Fixed:** a third fold. `train` / `dev` / `eval`, where `dev` is a held-out slice of train plus
**a separately recorded development noise tape**. Noise needs no labelling, so the second tape
costs one afternoon. FR-TST-7 makes the harness refuse `eval` without an explicit flag and stamp
the fold on every report (AC-100, AC-101) — because sealing by discipline does not survive a
debugging session at 2 a.m.

### 2. Segmentation is the one thing reprocessing cannot undo, and nothing said so · *new, CON-SEG-1*

§5.2 Principle 1 says every pass is a pure function of retained audio. §6.1 builds on it: tier
is a property of processing, not of the record, so a T0 capture reprocesses to a T3 result
(AC-39). The entire tier inversion — the spec's central structural idea — rests on this.

**Segmentation is the exception.** Only gated segments are retained; the stream between them is
discarded. A boundary error is therefore permanent: a transmission split in two stays split, a
merged pair stays merged, and a callsign clipped by insufficient pre-roll is gone from the
*audio*, not merely from the transcript. No later tier, no better model and no rig attached
afterwards recovers it.

Three documents asserted a general property that one subsystem violates, and none of them
noticed. It also means **AC-39 was testing a claim that is not unconditionally true** — the T0
and T3 results match only because segmentation happens to be tier-invariant, which nothing
required.

**Fixed:** CON-SEG-1 plus FR-SEG-7 (segmentation parameters SHALL NOT vary by tier — enforced
in the technical design by a segmenter that cannot accept a `Tier` argument), FR-SEG-8
(generous pre-roll *and* post-roll: retaining silence is cheap, discovering a clipped callsign
later is unrecoverable), FR-SEG-9 (optional continuous archive, the only mechanism that fully
closes it), AC-94..96, and risk R11.

### 3. The retention codec is an accuracy decision made two milestones before it can be measured · *new, CON-STO-1*

FR-STO-2 says store audio as Opus. FR-REP-4 says retained audio must be sufficient to re-run
**every** pass. Pass C is sub-phoneme acoustic discrimination on narrowband noisy speech — the
worst case for a perceptual codec tuned to discard exactly what is not perceptually salient.

The ordering is what makes this dangerous rather than merely arguable: **the codec is committed
in M2; Pass C is not measured until M4.** A lossy default would surface at M4 as *the core
accuracy thesis failing*, with nothing pointing at the storage layer — and M4's stated response
to that result is to delete Pass C and collapse the architecture. The project could have taken
its largest architectural decision on an artifact of a storage choice made two milestones
earlier for reasons of disk space.

**Fixed:** FR-STO-2a..c — lossless retention (FLAC, ~13 GB per year of daily 8-hour shifts)
until a lossy codec is justified by a measured comparison on the harness, and the measurement
that matters is its effect on **Pass C**, not Pass B (AC-102). Risk R10. Q13 opened for the
product decision; Q5 folded into it, since "how long to keep audio" is the easier half of the
question.

### 4. The work queue could not re-enqueue a pass · *technical design §7.1 — a real bug*

`UNIQUE(transmission_id, pass)` with `DONE` rows retained. Every reprocess in the product is
precisely the operation that constraint forbids: FR-REP-1, FR-REP-5, the lexicon-update re-run
of Pass D (FR-REP-6 — explicitly the cheapest and highest-value reprocess), and the whole
cross-tier flow (FR-REP-8..11, and AC-39, the acceptance gate for the tier inversion).

The failure mode is worse than an exception: nothing in §14 distinguishes "reprocess ran and
changed nothing" from "reprocess was silently refused."

**Fixed:** a partial unique index over active states only, `DONE` rows deleted on completion.
The queue is a queue; pass results and their fingerprints are the durable history.

### 5. No pass had an execution timeout · *new, FR-RUN-10a*

FR-RUN-9/10 and F18 handle a pass that **errors**. Nothing handled a pass that **hangs**. With
a single inference slot (technical design §4), one non-returning native call stops all
processing permanently while capture keeps filling the queue behind it — a live-looking app
with a frozen transcript and an unboundedly growing backlog. That is a worse failure than a
crash, because a crash is recovered by FR-RUN-8 and this is not recovered by anything.

**Fixed:** FR-RUN-10a, a deadline per lease proportional to segment duration and expected RTF,
with watchdog cancellation into normal `FAILED` handling (AC-99).

## Technical errors

| # | Finding | Severity | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **`:segment` declared `:core` as its only dependency**, but Silero VAD arrives via sherpa-onnx — the module as specified could not compile | Medium | `:onnx` module factored out. Keeps rule 2 clean: VAD is not ASR, and segmentation must not drag a transcription engine into its graph |
| T2 | **Capture assumed a 16 kHz input device.** Most USB Audio Class adapters — the intended input path — offer only 44.1/48 kHz, and `AudioRecord` fails at an unsupported rate rather than resampling | Medium | FR-CAP-2a, `deviceFormat`/`outputFormat` split, deterministic polyphase resampler in `:capture-api`, recorded on the session (AC-97) |
| T3 | **The FTS5 schema is not valid SQLite.** An external-content table was specified "where `is_current = 1`"; external content tables take no `WHERE` | Medium | Index the whole table, filter in the join. Also buys optional search over superseded text, which P9 argues for |
| T4 | **Determinism claimed without bound.** "Two harness runs must be byte-identical" does not hold across providers, chipsets or runtime versions — floating-point reduction order differs and the NPU path is quantized | Medium | Scoped to a fixed (machine, provider, threads, runtime version); every report carries them |
| T5 | **"ULIDs are safe to merge across export/import"** — ULIDs prevent id collisions; they say nothing about whether two databases' Stations or Voiceprints are the same entity | Low | Overclaim removed. | T7 | **Battery was an input to the shed controller feeding no output** — a signal that does nothing reads as covered and is worse than an absent one | Low | Below critical and not charging → level 4. The right response to a nearly-dead battery is to keep recording and stop thinking |

## Checked and found sound

Recorded so the next audit does not re-derive them:

- **The RTF convention** is applied consistently, and every carried-in figure is flagged where
  converted. `research/02`'s reversal by `research/04` §3 is explicit rather than silent, with
  the superseded reasoning left intact and its premise named — this is unusually good practice.
- **D5's boundary holds.** FR-ASR-16 (rerank a closed set) versus FR-DIG-4 (never emit a
  callsign) is a real distinction, correctly drawn, and AC-43 tests it adversarially.
- **The four attribution states are genuinely closed**, and `CORRECTED` is correctly a flag
  rather than a fifth state.
- **Requirement IDs are stable and unique**; no dangling references found.
- **NFR-1b** ("a weak device may know less; it may not be more wrong") is the load-bearing
  requirement of the tier model and is correctly reflected in P11 and AC-37.
- **§14A.2's split-by-session rule** is right, and its reasoning about three-way leakage
  (voice, channel, conversation) is correct. Finding 1 is a gap in it, not a fault in it.

## One thing the audit did not resolve

Every accuracy number in the entire document set is still transferred from ATC literature. The
audit improved the *measurement apparatus* — folds, calibration, determinism scoping, per-tier
reporting — and changed nothing about the fact that **no one has measured any of these models
on off-air amateur radio audio.** Q2 remains the only blocking question, and no further
document review will close it.

Record the tape.
